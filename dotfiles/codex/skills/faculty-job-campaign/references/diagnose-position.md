# Diagnosis and positioning

## Diagnose

1. Establish the target: role, department, country, research/teaching balance,
   essential and desirable criteria, deadline, and candidate constraints.
2. Give a brief simulated first read: identity, record, independence, and fit.
   This is a reading exercise, not a measured account of committee behaviour.
3. Map evidence to the actual criteria:

| Criterion and source | Candidate evidence and location | Assessment | Gap or unknown | Closable by deadline? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

When only a summary is supplied, cite it as the user-supplied summary and keep
that evidence limit visible.

Use met / partly evidenced / not documented / not met / needs verification.
Cover research trajectory, contributions and independence, funding where relevant,
teaching, supervision, service, and advertised eligibility. Explain disciplinary
conventions when assessing venues and authorship. Do not substitute journal metrics
for the substance of work or invent a publication-count cutoff.

4. Run a fair objection audit:

| Potential objection | Evidence; fair or speculative? | Clarify, fix, or accept | Concrete response |
|---|---|---|---|

Evaluate legitimate selection criteria. Do not endorse discriminatory speculation
or treat a contextual career-break statement as a weakness by itself.

5. Give a bounded verdict for each actual role: meets documented essentials /
   credible but material gaps / essential criterion unmet / insufficient evidence.
   Explain the main limiting factor if one is supported, or state that no single
   blocker is established. This assesses the case, not the probability of selection
   against an unknown applicant pool. A promising application can still be rejected.

## Position

Produce:

- **Identity line:** one intelligible sentence connecting a problem, contribution,
  and beneficiary or disciplinary audience.
- **Evidence:** two or three documented contributions that support it, with sources.
- **Home department fit:** explain how the profile answers this department's needs.
  For interdisciplinary work, show the bridge; do not force a false single identity.
- **Signature Research Idea (SRI), if useful:** a real research question, tool, or
  programme with a consistent descriptive name and accurate shared credit.
- **Alternatives:** when alternatives were considered, explain which claims or
  emphases fit less well. Do not invent two straw-man alternatives automatically.
- **Implication:** what leads in the CV, letter, and statement; what supports it.

Use principles 4–8 and 22. A claim of priority requires relevant literature or
other primary evidence. Narrowing a category until it sounds unique is not
verification. A plain, accurate description is sufficient without a novelty claim.
