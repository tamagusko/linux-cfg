# Asset review rubrics

## Shared pass and output

First check the user's request, advertisement constraints, audience, evidence,
contribution credit, and publication/funding status. Then apply the matching rubric.
Use principle numbers where they clarify the reasoning. Formatting or factual fixes
need not be forced into a numbered principle.

Default output:

- **Verdict:** ready for the stated use / fix before use / substantial rewrite /
  cannot assess without named evidence. A draft verdict does not authorize publishing.
- **Prioritised fixes:** location, issue, reason, and replacement wording where useful.
- **Unresolved claims:** identify the exact missing evidence. Keep placeholders
  visibly marked in working drafts; never present them as finished text.
- **Strengths:** at most two concrete observations, only when supported.

A narrow request receives a narrow edit. Do not expand a bio edit into a campaign
assessment, or diagnose the whole person from one post.

## CV framing

Check whether identity, relevant contributions, role-specific evidence, and dates
are easy to find. Explain collaborative roles and supervision accurately. Keep
submitted, accepted, and published work distinct. Do not require a slogan, grant
plan, or new section when the institution specifies a fixed CV format.

## Cover letter

Check the named role, explicit mapping to criteria, credible departmental fit, and
supported future contribution. Replace generic assertions with evidence. A standard
application opening is acceptable; specificity matters more than a forced hook.
Do not claim a course vacancy, collaborator agreement, or host commitment without
support. Keep the identity consistent in meaning without enforcing verbatim repetition.

## Research statement

Check a coherent programme, documented past contributions, feasible questions and
methods, dependencies, resources, uncertainty, and the candidate's own role. Verify
funding opportunities if named. Figures are optional and must fit page limits.
Several linked aims can be appropriate. Do not turn plausible future work into
promised results or remove scientifically necessary qualifications.

## Teaching statement

Check the connection between teaching philosophy and actual practice: courses,
audience, role, assessment, feedback, inclusion/accessibility, reflection, and local
curriculum fit. Distinguish assisted delivery from course ownership, proposed teaching
from delivered teaching, and student feedback from demonstrated learning outcomes.
Formal student evaluations are one possible source, not a universal requirement;
peer observations, assessed work, and documented teaching improvements can also
support effectiveness, with their limitations stated.
Do not invent teaching evaluations or require a national credential absent a criterion.

## Bio or public post

Check audience, length, current title and affiliation, useful substance, accurate
claims, attribution, and links. First or third person follows the requested context.
Apply the relevant playbook pattern if useful. No fixed promotion ratio, image, or
named SRI is necessary. An honest job-search announcement is not automatically a flaw.

## Talk abstract and job talk

Check a clear problem, contribution, supported findings, scope, specialist depth,
and fit to the time and audience. The abstract should promise only what the talk
contains. For a job talk, include future direction and local fit where expected.
Technical depth should remain explained, with useful figures and acknowledged
limitations. Do not demand a fixed nine-stage arc or repetition count. Prepare
substantive answers to likely questions, not evasive pivots.
