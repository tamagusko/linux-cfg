# Campaign playbook

## Plan within the available time

Use the user's horizon, defaulting to 90 days only for an open-ended campaign.
Work backward from application deadlines and reserve time for document production,
referee lead times, and final checks. Separate active effort from elapsed waiting.

| Priority | Action and completion evidence | Active effort | Expected hiring benefit and reason | Dependency | Due date | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use high / medium / low / uncertain for expected benefit, explained against an
actual gap. These are planning estimates, not selection probabilities. Decisions:
do now / after dependency / defer / skip. Include total effort and ensure it fits
the weekly budget. Sum row estimates with a calculator or code before claiming
the budget fits; reserve contingency or reduce scope when it does not. Principle
numbers are optional in the user-facing table. Identify the most valuable feasible action and a review date.
Do not require a skipped action when every proposed action fits the budget.

## Candidate actions, to be reprioritised by role

| Action | Typical active effort | Completion evidence or caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Map a vacancy to existing evidence | 1–2 hours | Every essential criterion has a source or explicit unknown |
| Clarify eligibility or a deadline | 30–60 minutes | Official source and check date; draft an inquiry if unresolved |
| Rework the CV opening and contribution statements | 2–4 hours | Claims trace to the record and match the target |
| Tailor the letter and statement | 1–2 days | Concrete department fit and feasible future programme |
| Assemble teaching and supervision evidence | Half to one day | Course, role, hours, student context, outcomes; avoid unsupported causal claims |
| Check a funding route where relevant | 1–3 hours | Current official call, candidate role, host requirements and open questions |
| Prepare a referee brief | 1–2 hours | Advert, deadline, submission method, CV, and accurate contribution examples |
| Improve one reusable research or teaching artefact | Days, estimate from scope | Working artefact with documentation and release rights checked |
| Prepare and rehearse a job talk | 1–3 days | Fits time limit; technical contribution and future fit understandable |
| Correct stale profiles and broken links | 1–3 hours | Consistent current facts; public edits require the requested authorization |
| Share a useful post or follow-up | 20–60 minutes | Specific audience and purpose; drafted text ready for review |

Avoid planning uncontrollable outcomes as tasks. Replace "land a flagship paper"
with controllable submission or revision milestones and show the review delay as
uncertain. Grants, accreditation, and relationships may extend beyond 90 days.
Branding, press, books, and public engagement are conditional: prioritise them when
the role values them or the user requests them, rather than banning them globally.

## Public assets

Choose the channel based on the target audience and existing presence. No platform
or posting frequency is presumed necessary. Five useful post patterns:

1. **Useful artefact:** what it does, who can use it, link, example, known limit.
2. **Method note:** a real technical choice, measured tradeoff, reproducible detail.
3. **Field read:** another group's contribution and what the evidence supports.
4. **Result comparison:** fair baseline, observed change, scope and uncertainty.
5. **Invitation:** audience, purpose, date, participation details, verified link.

Illustrative template: "We released [artefact] for [use]. On [evaluation], it
achieved [verified result]. [Known limitation]. Code and documentation: [link]."
Replace placeholders only with sourced facts. Do not copy numerical examples from
other campaigns. Length follows the channel and user's request, not a rigid quota.

Keep title, affiliation and status consistent across profiles. A bio can have
25-, 75-, and 150-word versions when useful. Photos are optional and depend on local
norms, accessibility, privacy preferences, and application instructions.

For a new paper: record its correct status, prepare a useful summary, link permitted
versions and artefacts, and update relevant talk material. Check embargoes and
release permissions before publication. Outreach lists should reflect genuine
relevance, not automatically every cited author. Drafting is separate from sending.

## Conferences and referees

Before a conference, identify a few relevant talks and genuine questions. After a
useful interaction, draft a specific follow-up; a later follow-up is optional,
depending on the response and shared work. Stop repeated unanswered outreach.

Choose referees who can substantiate the requested criteria. Follow the actual
number, independence requirements, and submission process in the advertisement.
Ask with reasonable lead time when possible; an urgent deadline does not require
months of small favours before a direct request. Supply evidence and allow referees
to make their own judgments. Do not manufacture superlatives or pressure them.

Use advertised channels for hiring inquiries. Do not seek confidential deliberations
or privately lobby selection-panel members. A campaign plan alone does not authorize
outreach. If a tracker is useful, keep it local and minimal: person, professional
context, purpose, deadline, authorized action, and response status.
