# Review rubrics

One rubric per asset type. Load the matching one in review mode.

Every rubric ends with the same output contract from SKILL.md: a verdict, then
numbered fixes keyed to rule numbers, each with the fixed line written out.

A universal first pass applies to every asset before the specific rubric:

**Universal pass**

1. Voice. Em dashes, hype adjectives, AI-marker constructions (SKILL.md voice
   section). Flag each occurrence with the replacement.
2. Rule 10. Orphan grammar anywhere: passive about your own career,
   circumstance-blaming, apologising for the record.
3. Rule 14. First sentence of each section: does it say what you did or what the
   reader gets?
4. Rule 1. Was this written from the reader's side? If the first paragraph is
   about the candidate's history rather than the reader's problem, it was not.

## CV top third

The part read in the first 90 seconds. Everything below the first page break is
verification, not persuasion.

| Check | Rule | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Identity line present, above the fold, one sentence | 4 | A paragraph listing four research areas |
| Home discipline anchor readable | 4 | A panel cannot tell which department hires this person |
| The lead strand leads | 7 | Two or three strands presented as equals |
| SRI named, and named identically wherever it appears | 8 | The same tool called three different things |
| Funding evidence findable without searching | 11 | Grants buried under projects, roles unstated, no named first-PI scheme |
| Independence evidence in the top third | diagnosis | Everything is the supervisor's project |
| Numbers where numbers exist | voice | "Extensive teaching experience" |
| At-a-glance line, if used, contains only checkable facts | 3 | A claim a panellist could challenge in the room |
| Venue framing matches the market | 11 adapted | Impact factors on a CV going to a DORA institution |

**The elimination pass.** Read the top third as a hostile panellist and write down
every objection that arrives within 30 seconds. That list is the real review
output (rule 3).

## Cover letter opening

The first paragraph decides whether the rest is read.

| Check | Rule | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Opens with what the department gets | 14 | "I am writing to apply for the position of..." |
| Anchors on something they already know | 5 | Opens with your own method name |
| Maps to the advertised criteria in their words | 2 | Your framing, not theirs |
| The search chair's sentence is present | 2 | Nothing addressed to the advertised gap |
| The head of school's sentence is present | 2 | No course you could take over, no money you could bring |
| One identity line, repeated from the CV verbatim | 4, 8 | A new formulation invented for this letter |
| Not one of the four bad proposals | 15 | Usually Social Security or Vague |

**Rewrite test.** Cover the letterhead. Could this paragraph be sent to any of
five departments? If yes, it is generic and it reads that way to them too.

## Research statement

Treat it as a white paper (rule 12). The book's format: sections with headings,
no more than a page and a half per section, a few paragraphs each, a figure with
a caption, written for someone with a doctorate outside your field, generous
white space. Not an exercise in filling a page limit.

| Check | Rule | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| One research question, matched to one tool that answers it | 15 | Five directions, none instrumented |
| Answers, not addresses or makes progress toward | 15 | Hedged outcome verbs throughout |
| Jenny Craig figure present | 13 | The reader has to construct the comparison |
| Family Portrait figure present | 13 | Related work as prose only, and nobody in the field is flattered |
| Butterfly figure at the top | 13 | Opens with a method diagram |
| Future-weighted, not past-weighted | 14 | Two thirds of the space on completed work |
| Funding plan named, with the scheme and eligibility window | 11 | "I will seek external funding" |
| SRI named and carried through | 8 | The through-line is implicit |
| White space, headings, figure captions that stand alone | 12 | A wall of text at the page limit |

**Which bad proposal is it?** Name one of the four (rule 15). Every weak
statement is one of them, and naming it produces the fix faster than a list of
small corrections.

Three lengths, all opening with the identity line (playbook, profile standards).

| Check | Rule | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| First clause is the identity line | 4 | Opens with the degree history |
| Reads in one breath at 25 words | 4 | Subordinate clauses |

| One concrete artefact named | 12 | Only abstractions |
| No hype adjectives | voice | "world-leading", "pioneering" |

## Job talk outline

Check against the arc (rules set E, Ch 11). The required shape:

```
1. Positioning open, working against the room's preconception of you
2. Beautiful Butterfly figure
3. Family Portrait figure, addressed to the experts
4. Zoom out: how you came to this problem, with the obstacle and the emotion
5. The break: the existing approach fails. Before half of the Jenny Craig figure
6. The SRI, named
7. Technical passage: fast, specialist-facing, deliberately not fully followable
8. After half of the Jenny Craig figure
9. What remains open
```

| Check | Rule | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Nine stages present and in order | E | Standard results-talk structure |
| A midpoint gear change exists | E | Uniformly accessible, which the book's author lost a job for |
| Depth spikes in the accessible half | E | Nothing for the local expert in the first twenty minutes |
| SRI named three times, once reserved for questions | E | Named once in the title |
| Key figure shown three times and left up during questions | E | Shown once |
| Three or four prepared points ready for the pivot | E | No question-period plan |
| Personal story present, with an obstacle | E | The generic sequence: got a grant, got data, fit a model, wrote it up |
| Fit slide: what you would teach, what you would apply for, who you would work with | 2 | Research only, nothing for the head of school |
