# Faculty Job Campaign

## Overview

A faculty job campaign is a marketing campaign whose only customer is a hiring
committee. Everything this skill produces serves one outcome: how search
committees, heads of school, panel members, and future referees perceive the
candidate.

Core principle, from the source book: **everything you get from other people
comes because you met someone else's needs.** A committee does not hire the best
scientist. It hires the candidate whose case is easiest to make in the room.

The 22 operational rules live in `rules.md`. Read it before any mode. The rules
are distilled from Marc Kuchner, *Marketing for Scientists* (Island Press, 2011),
each traceable to a chapter, with 2011-to-now adaptations marked.

## When to use

Trigger on:

- "am I competitive for X", "how do I look to a committee", "what is missing"
- "how should I position myself", "what is my research identity", "am I too scattered"
- "should I do X or Y before applying", "is this worth the time"
- "review this post / bio / statement / abstract / cover letter opening"
- "how do I get a letter from X", "what do I do at this conference"
- any faculty, lecturer, assistant professor, or tenure-track application

Do not use for: writing the actual manuscript (scientific-writing), choosing a
journal (journal-selection), rebuttals (review-response), or producing the
finished application documents where a repository already has a generator. This
skill decides *what the case is*; those tools execute it.

## The four modes

Pick the mode from the request. If the request spans several, run them in order:
diagnose, then position, then plan. Review is standalone.

| Mode | Input | Output contract |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnose** | CV plus target profile (fields, regions, institution tier) | The three-minute read, the gap table, the elimination audit, one verdict |
| **Position** | Diagnosis plus target profile | One identity line, two rejected alternatives with reasons |
| **Plan** | Diagnosis plus positioning | 90-day table, every row carrying effort and hiring impact |
| **Review** | A draft asset | Numbered fixes keyed to rule numbers, rewritten line where useful |

Each mode has a required output shape below. The shape is the deliverable. Do
not substitute prose for the table.

---

## Mode 1: Diagnose

Ask for the target profile if it is not given: fields, regions, institution tier,
and the timeframe. Without it, a diagnosis is unanchored. Everything else you
infer from the CV.

### Step 1: the three-minute read

A committee member opens the file for about three minutes before the shortlist
meeting. Reproduce that read as four timed passes. For each pass, state what the
reader actually sees, not what the candidate meant.

```
0:00-0:30  IDENTITY   name, current title and institution, the one line they
                      would repeat. If there is no one line, say so plainly:
                      that is the finding.
0:30-1:30  RECORD     publication scan. First-author count, venue tier as the
                      target market reads it, recency, whether the list coheres
                      into one strand or scatters.
1:30-2:30  INDEPENDENCE  evidence of a researcher who can run a group: grants
                      held, own students, a named thing that is theirs, papers
                      without the doctoral supervisor, invitations, service.
2:30-3:00  FIT        teaching evidence, service, and match to the gap the
                      advertisement is trying to fill. Plus legal and mobility
                      facts a panel checks early.
```

### Step 2: the gap table

Against the criteria that market actually applies. Required columns, no
substitutes:

| Criterion | What the CV shows | What the tier expects | Gap | Closable before next cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover at minimum: research trajectory, venue standing, independence, funding
capacity, teaching, supervision, service and esteem, fit and mobility.

### Step 3: the elimination audit (rule 3)

Committees eliminate before they select, and a single objection outweighs
several positives. List every objection a hostile panellist could raise in
30 seconds, and for each: pre-empt, fix, or accept and route around.

| Objection as it would be spoken in the room | Fair? | Response |
|---|---|---|

### Step 4: the verdict

One paragraph. It must name the single binding blocker, not a list. Then a
shortlist judgement per tier in the target market:

```
Tier A (name the institutions): <shortlistable now / borderline / not yet>
Tier B: ...
Binding blocker: <one thing>
```

### Honesty discipline for this mode

This is the one place where prohibition applies, because flattery is the failure
mode and it is a defect, not a courtesy.

**Red flags. If you write one of these, delete it and redo the item:**

- praise with no artefact behind it ("strong publication record")
- a gap named and then softened in the same sentence
- "well positioned", "compelling", "impressive", "unique blend"
- a verdict with no named blocker
- a gap table row where "Gap" is empty because the criterion was chosen to flatter
- hedging a tier judgement so it cannot be wrong

Every claim in a diagnosis points at a line of the CV or at its absence. A
finding you cannot source is not a finding.

## Mode 2: Position

Apply rules 4 to 8 and rule 22. Output exactly this shape:

```
IDENTITY LINE
  "<one sentence, the form: the one who does X for Y>"

WHY IT HOLDS
  Rule 4 (owns a phrase):        <the phrase, and who else owns it>
  Rule 5 (anchors on the known): <what the committee already knows>
  Rule 6 (first in category):    <the category, and why first is defensible>
  Rule 7 (does not dabble):      <what leads, what becomes supporting evidence>
  Rule 8 (SRI):                  <the named thing, and where the name appears>
  Rule 22 (go-to test):          <could a panellist who never met you say it?>

REJECTED
  "<alternative 1>" - <why it fails, by rule number>
  "<alternative 2>" - <why it fails, by rule number>

HOME DISCIPLINE ANCHOR
  <which department hires this person, and what stops them reading as
   neither-one-thing-nor-the-other>
```

The anchor line is not optional for interdisciplinary candidates. It is the 2026
adaptation on rule 4 and the most common way a hybrid profile loses a shortlist.

## Mode 3: Plan

A 90-day campaign, front-loaded to the next two application cycles. Not a
five-year brand project.

Required table. Both scoring columns are mandatory fields, so an unranked
recommendation cannot be produced:

| # | Action | Rule | Effort | Hiring impact | Verdict | By when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Effort**: hours, days, or weeks. Real units, not "low/medium/high".
- **Hiring impact**: high, medium, low, none. High means it changes whether a
  shortlist happens in the next two cycles.
- **Verdict**: `Do now`, `Do after X`, `Skip, low hiring impact`, `Skip, wrong stage`.

`Skip, low hiring impact` is a correct and expected verdict. A plan with no
skipped actions has not been prioritised. The catalogue in `playbook.md` carries
the standing impact ranking and the worked examples; pull actions from there
rather than inventing generic ones.

Close the plan with:

```
THE ONE THING
  If only one action happens in 90 days, it is: <action>. Because: <blocker it clears>.
```

Every action must be concrete enough to check off. "Be more visible" is not an
action. "Post the archetype-3 method note on the CAMINA release, 400 words, one
figure" is an action.

## Mode 4: Review

Take the draft asset. Identify its type, load the matching rubric from
`review-rubrics.md`, and return:

```
VERDICT: <ship / fix then ship / rewrite>

FIXES
  1. [Rule N] <what is wrong> -> <the fixed line, written out>
  2. ...

WHAT IT DOES WELL   (only if true, maximum two items, each pointing at a line)
```

Rewrite the line rather than describing the fix, wherever a line can be
rewritten. A review that only diagnoses costs the candidate a second round.

## Voice

Committee-credible register. The reader is a professor who reads a hundred of
these and distrusts salesmanship.

- No em dashes. Use a comma, a colon, a full stop, or parentheses.
- No hype adjectives: cutting-edge, world-class, pioneering, ground-breaking,
  transformative, unparalleled, compelling, impressive, exciting, passionate.
- No AI-marker constructions: "delve into", "it is worth noting that",
  "navigate the landscape of", "leverage" as a verb for ordinary use, "robust"
  as a synonym for good, "underscores", "showcases", "highlights the importance
  of", "in today's rapidly evolving", "not only X but also Y" as a rhythm,
  triples for emphasis, "-ing" clauses that restate the sentence just written.
- No rhetorical questions in candidate-facing prose.
- Concrete nouns and named artefacts. "A 30-hour doctoral course at IME" beats
  "significant teaching experience".
- Numbers where numbers exist. Ranges where they do not. Never a vague
  quantifier standing in for a number you could look up.

## What this skill deliberately does not recommend

Kept here so the skill does not quietly reintroduce them. Each was in the source
book and each is low hiring impact for a faculty campaign:

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| A personal logo | The book itself warns that a lone scientist with a logo reads as self-promoting (Ch 5). Project and tool logos are fine. |
| Science rap, viral video (Ch 12) | Reaches the public, not panels. |
| Press releases, media training, being a quotable pundit (Ch 13) | Serves institutional communications, not shortlisting. The sound-bite craft transfers to interview answers and is kept there. |
| Congressional lobbying, public mythbusting, citizen-science advocacy (Ch 13-14) | Out of scope for hiring. |
| Writing a trade book | Multi-year, and the return lands after the campaign window. |

If a candidate asks about one of these, say `Skip, low hiring impact` and name
the higher-impact action that competes for the same hours.
